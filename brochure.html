PHYSICS ENGINE
Core Models
Yamamura–Bohdansky Sputtering Yield
Y(E) = Q · S_n*(E) · (1 − (E_th / E)^(2/3))^0.25
Semi-empirical yield formula valid from threshold to keV regime. Q is a material-dependent constant encoding binding energy and mass ratio. S_n*(E) is the reduced nuclear stopping cross-section computed from the universal Lindhard–Scharff–Schiott (LSS) function. E_th ≈ U_s / γ where γ = 4M_1M_2/(M_1+M_2)² is the kinematic factor. Implemented for all 8 target materials with tabulated Q, U_s, E_th values.
Cosine-Power Emission Distribution
f(θ) ∝ cos^n(θ) where n = 1 + 0.6 · (E/500)^0.4
Angular distribution of sputtered atoms from the target surface under normal Ar⁺ incidence. Pure cosine (n = 1) at low ion energies transitions to overcosine (n > 1) at higher energies, consistent with molecular dynamics and experimental observations. Controls the radial deposition footprint and uniformity profile on the substrate.
Thompson–Sigmund Energy Distribution
f(E_s) ∝ E_s / (E_s + U_s)³ · (1 − √((E_s + U_s) / E_max))
Energy spectrum of sputtered atoms at the target surface, derived from the linear collision cascade model. U_s is the surface binding energy. E_max = γE − U_s is the maximum transferable energy. Sampled stochastically each frame to assign initial kinetic energies to emitted atom packets.
Mean Free Path — Kinetic Theory
λ = kT / (√2 · π · d_Ar² · P)
Hard-sphere kinetic theory MFP for Ar at process temperature T and pressure P. d_Ar = 3.7 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K. Evaluated per frame from current pressure and gas mixture (Ar + O₂). The scattering ratio d/λ drives the transport regime classification: Ballistic (d/λ < 0.5), Moderate (0.5–2.0), High (> 2.0). Mixed Ar/O₂ atmospheres use a mole-fraction-weighted cross-section.
Lorentz E×B Electron Drift — Magnetron Principle
v_drift = (E × B) / B² r_L = m_e · v_⊥ / (e · B)
Secondary electrons emitted from the target are trapped in crossed electric and magnetic fields, executing cycloidal drift trajectories above the target surface. The closed drift path defines the racetrack erosion groove. Increased ionisation in the racetrack region raises local plasma density, sustaining discharge at lower pressures (1–10 mTorr) than a diode configuration.
Particle Transport Implementation
Ion Dynamics

Ar⁺ ions spawn at the plasma–sheath boundary, accelerate toward the cathode under the sheath electric field with E×B lateral wobble. Impact is detected via a swept collision check (no tunnelling at high simulation speed). Ion energy at impact determines sputtering yield and ejected atom energy via Thompson sampling.

Atom Transport & Deposition

Sputtered atom packets travel upward with cosine-sampled angle and Thompson-sampled energy. Each frame computes scattering probability from λ. Successful substrate impacts are distributed across bins via a Gaussian kernel with racetrack shoulder rings. Sticking coefficient applied probabilistically. Particle arrays are ceiling-bounded to prevent frame rate collapse.

Reactive & Co-Sputtering Capabilities
Reactive Sputtering (Ar / O₂)

O₂ partial pressure modifies target poisoning level dynamically. Sputtering yield decreases as oxide forms on the target surface. Hysteresis indicator shows that return to metallic mode requires O₂ fraction below the onset value — faithfully reproducing the Berg hysteresis loop. RF mode shows reduced poisoning sensitivity relative to DC.

Co-Sputtering — 3 Independent Guns

Up to three guns operate simultaneously with independent material and power settings. Film composition is tracked proportionally using per-source yield values. Alloy fraction readout updates in real time. Demonstrates why equal power does not equal equal composition due to yield differences — a key industrial concept for ITO, Cu-Al, and multi-component alloy deposition.

5 Structured Virtual Lab Modules
LAB 01
Pressure vs. Transport Regime
Vary working pressure from 1.5 to 18 mTorr and observe the transition from ballistic to high-scattering transport. Measure deposition rate, scattering ratio d/λ, and radial uniformity at each pressure point. Export time-series CSV and identify the crossover pressure where d/λ = 1.
Variable: Pressure~30 minCSV export required
LAB 02
Sputtering Yield vs. Power & Material
Compare yield response across Cu, Al, Zn, W, and Ta as gun power increases from 100 W to 500 W. Quantify the role of binding energy and mass ratio in determining yield. Identify which material shows the steepest power-yield gradient and explain using the Yamamura formula.
Variable: Power + Material~35 minMulti-run comparison
LAB 03
Geometry Optimisation for Uniformity
Systematically vary target diameter, substrate diameter, and target–substrate distance to minimise non-uniformity percentage. Record the radial profile CSV at each condition. Identify the geometry combination that achieves the best uniformity and rationalise using the cosine emission footprint.
Variable: Geometry~40 minRadial profile analysis
LAB 04
Reactive Sputtering Hysteresis
Increase O₂ fraction incrementally and record yield and deposition rate. Identify the poisoning onset point. Then reduce O₂ and record the return path. Map the full hysteresis loop and compare DC vs. RF mode behaviour. Discuss implications for process control in TiN and ZnO deposition.
Variable: O₂ fraction~35 minHysteresis mapping
LAB 05
Co-Sputtering Composition Control
Use two and three active guns (Cu + Al, then Cu + Al + Zn) and vary relative power settings. Predict the expected film composition from yield ratios before each run. Compare prediction to simulator output. Determine the power ratio required to achieve a 50:50 Cu:Al film.
Variable: Multi-gun power~40 minComposition tracking

FILM GROWTH MODES & FEATURES
Three Implemented Growth Modes
Frank–van der Merwe

Layer-by-layer growth. High surface mobility. Each monolayer completes before the next nucleates. Produces atomically flat, epitaxial films. Simulator shows sequential layer filling with uniform substrate coverage.

Condition: E_adsorption > E_adhesion · Lattice mismatch ~ 0%

Volmer–Weber

Island growth. Low surface mobility. Atoms prefer bonding to each other over the substrate. Random 3D islands nucleate and grow before coalescence. Simulator renders discrete nucleation sites that merge into a continuous film.

Condition: E_adhesion > E_adsorption · High surface energy mismatch

Stranski–Krastanov

Layer then islands. Flat wetting layers form first, then accumulated strain from lattice mismatch drives 3D island nucleation. Simulator transitions mid-run at the critical thickness controlled by the lattice mismatch slider.

Condition: Small lattice mismatch initially · Strain energy accumulates

Key Pedagogical Features
AI Recipe Advisor

An integrated advisory engine evaluates the current parameter set and flags configurations unlikely to produce a physically meaningful result. It suggests corrected recipes with predicted transport regime, uniformity percentage, and growth mode. Students compare their own prediction to the advisor output — a structured pre-lab exercise in process reasoning.

Thickness Target with Auto-Pause

Students set a target film thickness in nm. A real-time progress bar tracks deposition toward the target. The simulator auto-pauses when the target is reached and displays an achievement summary: final thickness, elapsed time, uniformity percentage, and average deposition rate. Resume continues deposition beyond the target.

CSV Data Export

Three export formats: time-series log (rate, yield, MFP, uniformity per second), radial thickness profile (binned substrate cross-section), and process summary (all conditions at end of run). Enables quantitative post-session data analysis, graphing assignments, and direct comparison across experimental conditions.

Instructor Guide Download

A one-click plain-text instructor guide generates dynamically from the lab module database. It includes learning objectives, expected trends, procedure steps, reflection questions with model answers, and a 10-mark rubric per module. Generated at runtime — always matches the current version of the lab modules.

Real-World Reference Callouts

Each of the 14 tutorial chapters contains a highlighted callout box linking the simulated concept to a published experimental result or industry practice, with a peer-reviewed citation. Examples include the IBM Cu interconnect paper (Edelstein 1999), the Berg reactive sputtering hysteresis paper (1988), and the Thornton zone model (1974).

Reveal-on-Demand Expected Results

Each lab module contains a hidden "What you should observe" panel that students unlock after completing their own observations. The panel provides the expected trend, the quantitative crossover value (e.g. d/λ = 1 at ~6 mTorr), and the physical mechanism in one paragraph. Prevents premature answer exposure while providing immediate feedback after the experiment.

Process Diagnostics Panel

Live status bar displays: discharge voltage (V) · ion current density (mA/cm²) · ion energy (eV) · sputtering yield (atoms/ion) · illustrative yield (transport-corrected) · mean free path λ (cm) · scattering ratio d/λ · transport regime label · deposition rate (arb. units/s) · film thickness (nm) · non-uniformity (%) · growth mode · sticking coefficient · total process time. All values update at the chart refresh rate (approximately every 1 second of simulation time).

Model Limitations

This simulator is built for learning and intuition, not fab-grade process fitting. The discharge model is a parametric fit — not a full PIC or fluid plasma solver. Atom trajectories use a reduced-order cosine emission and Thompson energy sampler, not molecular dynamics or SRIM/TRIM. Deposition rate and film thickness numbers are scaled for classroom time using internal calibration constants and will not match absolute fab measurements. The illustrative yield applies empirical transport and sticking factors on top of the Yamamura formula — treat it as a trend indicator, not a quoted yield. Students moving to real sputter tools should treat all absolute numbers as qualitative guides only.
