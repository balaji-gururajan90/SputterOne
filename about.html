About the Simulation Model

This sputtering simulator is designed as an interactive educational tool for understanding the core physical processes of magnetron sputtering. It bridges the gap between theoretical plasma physics and visual intuition by simulating processes in real-time right in your browser.

Physics Engine Breakdown
Core mechanisms simulated in real-time
01 Plasma Generation & Confinement
Electron Impact Ionization

The simulation begins by modeling the background Argon gas at a specific pressure. When high negative voltage is applied to the cathode (target), free electrons are accelerated away from it. These energetic electrons collide with neutral Argon atoms (Ar), stripping away outer electrons to create positive Argon ions (Ar+) in a process called electron impact ionization.

Fig 1. Current chamber view showing the updated substrate, transport region, plasma zone, and target layout

To increase ionization efficiency, a magnetic field (B) is introduced behind the target. In this simulator, magnetic confinement is represented with a reduced-order racetrack model rather than full Lorentz-force trajectory integration. The result is a dense localized plasma region near the target surface, which increases the ionization probability of Argon gas and thus the sputtering rate.

02 Target Interaction
Yamamura Yield Mechanics

Once created, the positive Ar+ ions are accelerated across the cathode sheath and crash into the target surface. The physical ejection of target atoms is modeled using a reduced-order Yamamura/Bohdansky-style sputtering yield fit with material-dependent binding energy, threshold energy, and ion-energy scaling.

The simulation calculates the Sputtering Yield (Y) - the average number of sputtered atoms per incident ion. This calculation now follows a Yamamura-inspired energy dependence, combining projectile-target mass transfer, a stopping-power term, a threshold-energy correction, and the surface binding energy of the target material.

This is why switching target materials (e.g., from Copper to Tungsten) in the simulator immediately alters the visual ejection rate and the calculated yield metrics, reflecting their different atomic masses, threshold behaviour, and binding energies. A bounded angle correction is also applied so the yield remains responsive to the reduced-order ion incidence geometry.

03 Deposition & Film Growth
Transport and Mean Free Path

Ejected target atoms leave the surface with a kinetic energy of a few eV and travel through the vacuum chamber towards the substrate. The simulation uses a simplified Thompson-like energy sampler together with pressure-based gas transport through the concept of Mean Free Path (MFP, lambda).

Fig 2. Flat radial thickness profiles and transport metrics across working pressure

At high pressures, sputtered atoms frequently collide with background Argon gas, losing energy and scattering (thermalization). At low pressures, they experience fewer collisions, retaining their energy and directionality. The simulator dynamically visualizes this transport process, demonstrating how adjusting chamber pressure affects the resulting thin film's uniformity and deposition rate on the substrate.

Technical Implementation
Browser-Based Simulation Stack

The entire physics engine is built using vanilla JavaScript and rendered in real-time using HTML5 Canvas APIs, requiring no external plugins or server-side computations. It combines reduced-order discharge estimates, current-linked ion spawning, pressure-dependent transport, and simplified film-growth logic in a browser-friendly framework. The visual plasma glow and chamber effects are programmatic, keeping the simulator lightweight and responsive across modern browsers.

Model Limitations
This simulator is built for learning and intuition, not fab-grade process fitting. Key approximations to be aware of:

Vacuum Environment: SputterOne models the working gas environment only. The simulator assumes the chamber has been evacuated to a base pressure of approximately 1×10⁻⁶ Torr before Ar backfill, consistent with typical turbomolecular-pumped magnetron sputtering systems. Residual gas contamination (H₂O, O₂, N₂, CO at partial pressures of 10⁻⁷–10⁻⁸ Torr) and outgassing effects are outside the scope of the reduced-order model. Users requiring film purity estimates should consult vacuum system design references and residual gas analysis data from their specific chamber.

(1) The discharge model (voltage, current, ion energy) is a parametric fit tuned to qualitative trends — it is not a full PIC or fluid plasma solver.
(2) Atom trajectories use a reduced-order cosine emission + Thompson energy sampler, not molecular dynamics or SRIM/TRIM.
(3) Deposition rate and film thickness numbers are scaled for classroom time using internal calibration constants (DEPOSITION_UNITS_PER_LANDING, THICKNESS_NM_PER_PROFILE_UNIT) and will not match absolute fab measurements.
(4) The "Illustrative Yield" shown in the UI applies empirical transport and sticking factors on top of the Yamamura formula — treat it as a trend indicator, not a quoted yield.
Students moving to real sputter tools should treat all absolute numbers as qualitative guides only.
